## Interpretation

- CodeNet: SPECTRA has 79.91% Accuracy versus DeepSim 81.33%. C4-adapted provisionally reports 92.66%, but is incomplete and excluded from the completed ranking. Retain this result when evaluating claims.
- AtCoder: SPECTRA has 76.10% Accuracy versus RtvNN 82.91%, a 6.81 percentage-point gap. SPECTRA trained for 66 epochs and selected epoch 56; this is not a four-epoch or session-budget stop.
- BigCloneBench: SPECTRA has **67.92% F1** versus FA-AST-GGNN **98.27% F1**, a 30.35-point gap. Its 91.37% Accuracy is not the primary result because only 924/7,129 validation pairs are positive. SPECTRA selected epoch 10 and stopped at epoch 20.
- These results do not support a general SPECTRA superiority claim. A low score alone does not establish an implementation bug. Input representation, encoder, objective and readout differ across baseline families, so the gap cannot be attributed solely to spectral pooling.
- BigCloneBench validation contains only 127 unique programs despite 7,129 pairs; test contains 128. Near-perfect graph-baseline results need this limited independent support disclosed. Pair count is not the number of independent programs, and these scores alone do not prove leakage.

## Integrity and numerical behavior

The eleven supplied current/best models loaded strictly into their matching local architectures. Model/optimizer/history tensors are finite; archived training sources match the current training fingerprint. Dataset hashes independently match the released v2 bundles. Validation support, confusion-matrix metrics, best-epoch selection, cursor/update counts and patience stops are consistent. All eleven stopped on validation patience and have test evaluation off.

AtCoder SPECTRA logged two FP32 fallback recoveries, alongside AMP retries. Both fallback groups recovered, final states are finite, and no fatal numerical artifact was found. These are successful replay events, not evidence that batches were skipped. The benchmark checks passed, but timings exclude loading/transfers/optimizer/checkpoint I/O and use different physical batches across methods.

AUROC/AP were range/consistency checked but cannot be independently reproduced without validation predictions. Checkpoint integrity does not establish exact original-paper baseline fidelity or statistical superiority.

## Read-only input diagnostics

All 127 BigCloneBench validation graphs loaded and had AST and projected DDG edges. However, just **3 of 17,111 nodes (0.0175%)** had nonempty node lexical features. A deterministic 512-program training sample was similarly sparse: 18/63,737 nodes (0.0282%). The corresponding AtCoder validation sample had nonempty lexical features on 11.70% of nodes. The full model disables source-level lexical features, so that channel does not compensate for sparse node features.

This suggests a possible representation bottleneck, not a demonstrated cause or a confirmed implementation bug. BigCloneBench had 122 distinct input signatures among 127 validation programs, with a largest identical-input group of four; its inputs have not all collapsed to one graph. Canonical unknown types were below 0.1% of validation nodes. Twenty-one validation graphs reached the 256-node cap; this count alone does not establish truncation. Diagnostics inspect inputs, not new training or test predictions.
